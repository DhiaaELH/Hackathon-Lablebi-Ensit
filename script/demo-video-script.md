# LessonForge demo video: script

A 3-minute motion-design demo of [lablebi.nerio.dev](https://lablebi.nerio.dev) aimed at students.

- **Length:** 2:59 · 1920×1080 · 30 fps
- **Voice:** English, AI neural voice (Microsoft edge-tts, `en-US-AvaMultilingualNeural`), with word-by-word captions
- **Music:** synthesized beat (no samples); the drop lands on the logo reveal, and the music ducks under the voice
- **Footage:** real screen recordings of the live site (seeded fictional accounts), with an animated cursor, click ripples and camera zooms timed to the narration
- **Style:** the site's hand-drawn "Sketch" look: dotted paper, Kalam headings, ink borders, red `#ff4d4d`, post-it `#fff9c4`

Timestamps are where each scene starts in the final cut.

---

## 1. Hook · 0:00

**On screen:** dark night scene. A clock reads 23:47, "exam tomorrow, 8:30". A stack of slides falls in while a counter runs to "200 slides". A blurry scanned handout slides in (`scan_final(3).pdf`). Speech bubbles pop up in three languages: "Clé étrangère ??", "شنوّة هذا؟", "wait… what?". Everything blurs back as "Where do you even **start**?" lands, with "start" circled.

> It's the night before the exam. Two hundred slides. A blurry scanned handout. Notes in three languages. Where do you even start?

## 2. Logo · 0:12

**On screen:** circle transition to paper. The red "L" mark spins in and the letters of **LessonForge** bounce in. The tagline appears with "*finally*" in red and a scribbled underline. The music drops here.

> Meet LessonForge. Your course notes, finally alive.

## 3. Upload → AI team · 0:18

**On screen:** post-it "Upload anything". File cards pop in on their words: Slides, PDF notes, Handout photos, YouTube lecture. They fly into a spinning "AI team" hub. Agent cards light up one by one with progress bars: Course analysis, Exercises, Quizzes, Mock exam, Revision pack. They connect to a "Quality check" node, and a green **✓ verified** stamp slams on "every single piece". A "Course ready ✓" post-it pops, and the camera zooms through it into the site.

> Just upload your course. Slides, PDFs, photos of handouts, even a YouTube lecture. A team of specialist AI agents reads it, builds clean chapters, and writes exercises, quizzes, a mock exam and a revision pack. Then a quality checker reviews every single piece.

## 4. ① Your course, structured & cited · 0:39

**On screen (site):** Library → open *Bases de données relationnelles et SQL*. Zoom on the course header, then scroll through chapter 1 (objectives, key terms, hard points). The cursor clicks the `S2` citation chip, and the camera zooms into the source excerpt that opens.

> A few minutes later, your course is ready. Chapters, objectives, key ideas, no more digging through two hundred slides. And every section cites the exact page or slide it came from, so you can always check the source.

## 5. ② Ask Nova, your course assistant · 0:56

**On screen (site):** click "Ask Nova" and the side panel opens. Type *"Why do we need foreign keys? Give me an example."* and send. "Nova is thinking…", then Sam (the tutor) answers with an example from the course and an `S2` citation. Nova goes full screen and the camera follows the answer.

> Stuck on a concept? Ask Nova, your course assistant. Nova sends your question to the right specialist, and the answer comes only from your own course, never from thin air. In English, French, Arabic, or even Tunisian Derja.

## 6. ③ Practice for real · 1:14

**On screen (site):** Practice tab with progress rings per section. Open "1.1 Relations, attributs et clés", then pick an answer in the quick questions. Scroll to the full TD-style exercise and reveal hint 1, then hint 2. A partial answer is typed and graded by AI: **65%**, with a rubric (met / missing) and the misconception it spotted ("Incomplete answer omitting the question about NULL values…").

> Then, practice for real. Not just multiple choice: full exercises, like in your tutorials, with progressive hints, AI grading against a rubric, and a worked solution once you've tried. Got it wrong? It tells you which misconception tripped you up.

## 7. ④ Revise smarter · 1:33

**On screen (site):** Revision tab. A summary with citations (zoom), then Flashcards: a card flips to its answer, then a second one. Review mode, then a hover over "Offline pack" and "PDF".

> Revision time? Get chapter summaries, flashcards with spaced repetition, a revision planner, read-aloud, a clean PDF to print, and even an offline pack for the bus ride home.

## 8. ⑤ See what you really know · 1:47

**On screen (site):** Topic map, zoomed in. Click "Normalisation" and its prerequisite chain lights up ("builds on: Dépendances fonctionnelles"). Then click "Requêtes de sélection et jointures".

> The topic map links every concept to what it builds on, and colors it by how well you know it. So you always know what to work on next.

## 9. ⑥ Never study alone · 1:57

**On screen (site), three quick cuts with a tag on each:**
- **Community:** the feed of chapter threads, then the "WHERE ou HAVING ?" thread with the tutor's accepted answer
- **Past papers:** the 2025 exam with corrections and the chapter-frequency chart
- **Challenges & XP:** level card and challenges with XP rewards, then the Leaderboard tab

> And you're never studying alone. Ask questions on each chapter, join study groups with an exam countdown, find past exam papers with real corrections, and take on challenges from your teachers to earn XP and climb the leaderboard.

## 10. ⑦ For teachers: anonymous insights · 2:13

**On screen (site, teacher account):** Class insights. Zoom on "Numbers appear only when at least 5 different students contributed. Signals carry no account id or name." Scroll the confusion heatmap, then zoom on a "hidden (<5)" cell.

> Teachers get class insights: where the class gets stuck, section by section, without ever revealing who. Fewer than five students? It stays hidden.

> It speaks your languages: English, French, Arabic, right to left included, and Derja. And it fits in your pocket.
